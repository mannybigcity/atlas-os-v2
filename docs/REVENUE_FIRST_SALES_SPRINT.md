# Atlas Revenue-First Sales Sprint

Prepared: July 17, 2026

## Immediate objective

Earn the next paid Atlas engagement without adding another major feature.

Seven-day target:

- finish one visible QTime outcome;
- start three personalized local-business conversations;
- book one 20-minute revenue-leak review; and
- close one paid, tightly scoped 30-day founding pilot.

Nothing in this sprint authorizes automatic or bulk outreach. Manny approves
each business, channel, and final message before it is sent.

## The offer to sell

Working name: **Atlas 30-Day Revenue Leak Sprint**

Promise: Atlas helps the owner choose one place where inquiries, estimates,
bookings, memberships, repeat visits, or follow-up may be getting lost. Atlas
then installs the smallest practical system around that one issue and reviews
what changed after 30 days.

Included:

1. A focused business and revenue-leak assessment.
2. One agreed 30-day goal and success measure.
3. One private Atlas workspace.
4. A simple opportunity/follow-up pipeline.
5. One approved follow-up sequence or one focused marketing asset set.
6. Weekly owner check-ins and a day-30 review.

Not included:

- autonomous customer contact or publishing;
- unlimited content, research, or consulting;
- ad spend, paid data, or outside software fees;
- guaranteed leads, sales, revenue, or business results; or
- multiple business problems in the same sprint.

## Founding-pilot price recommendation

Offer the first three new businesses a **$300 paid 30-day pilot**, collected
before work begins. This is an early proof price, not permanent public pricing.
At day 30, quote continued support separately based on the actual work and API
costs. Do not promise a $300 renewal before the first sprint is measured.

Why this price:

- it is large enough to prove that the problem is worth paying to solve;
- it is still accessible for an owner-led local business;
- the narrow scope protects Manny's time and health; and
- three initial pilots can produce $900 and the proof needed to raise pricing.

The $6,000 monthly milestone will not come from twenty labor-heavy $300
accounts. The proof phase should lead toward a smaller number of higher-value,
repeatable engagements. For example, twelve $500 monthly clients or six $1,000
monthly clients both reach $6,000, but pricing should only rise after the work,
capacity, and customer value are measured.

## The sales conversation

The 20-minute call should answer only five things:

1. Where does a new inquiry, booking, estimate, or member come from?
2. What should happen next?
3. Where does the owner currently lose visibility or time?
4. What one measurable result would matter in the next 30 days?
5. Is that problem important enough to fund a $300 focused pilot now?

Close plainly:

> Based on what you told me, I recommend a 30-day Atlas pilot focused only on
> [problem]. The founding-pilot price is $300, paid before we start. You will see
> the plan, approve every external action, and we will review [measure] at day
> 30. If it is not a fit, there is no automatic renewal.
