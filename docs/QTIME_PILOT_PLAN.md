## Proposed first outcome

Each week, QTIME receives:

- one reviewed content plan;
- a small set of verified growth opportunities;
- three clear priority actions;
- a simple follow-up list; and
- one short check-in from Manny inside its private Atlas workspace.

This is a human-assisted service delivered through Atlas. Atlas does not yet
autonomously research, publish, contact leads, or make business decisions.

## Access gate

Complete every item before giving Quincy credentials or entering his business
data:

- MFA enabled for Atlas administrator accounts covering Supabase, GitHub,
  Netlify, email, Resend, and the domain provider where available
- Quincy created as a separate Supabase Auth user
- QTIME created as a separate organization
- Quincy connected only to QTIME with an `owner` membership
- Sign in as Quincy and confirm only QTIME data is visible
- Confirm Atlas Test Organization data is not visible
- Confirm `/lions-den` denies Quincy safely
- Sign in as Super Admin and confirm QTIME is visible from The Lion's Den
- Record the date and result of the isolation test

Stop onboarding immediately if any access check fails.

## Minimum intake

Collect only information needed to deliver the pilot:

- Preferred customer and company names
- Time zone and preferred check-in day
- One-sentence business description
- Primary 30-day revenue or operating goal
- Current offer and target customer
- Top three priorities
- Biggest current bottleneck
- Active marketing platforms
- Brand voice and topics to avoid
- Content approval preference
- Current prospect/follow-up method

Quincy should enter business context after signing in whenever practical.
Never ask for passwords, payment-card data, government IDs, or unrelated
confidential records.

## First seven days

### Day 1: confirm the agreement

- Reconcile the original payment and promise.
- Explain that this is a founding, human-assisted pilot.
- Confirm the first weekly outcome and communication cadence.

### Day 2: provision and test access

- Complete the access gate.
- Send the production password-recovery flow so Quincy chooses his password.
- Confirm login and logout without asking Quincy for his password.

### Day 3: complete intake

- Capture the approved business profile and 30-day goal.
- Record three priorities and the current follow-up method.

### Days 4-5: deliver the first useful package

- A seven-day reviewed content plan for the project Quincy selects
- A practical sponsorship one-sheet for Roll'n Wars if sponsorship is the
  selected revenue goal
- Three verified growth or sponsor opportunities with source and relevance
  notes
- Three priority actions with owner and due date
- A simple sponsor/prospect follow-up list with next step and status

Manual research and review are acceptable. Label assumptions and do not invent
facts, metrics, prospects, or results.

### Days 6-7: review and learn

- Walk Quincy through the package.
- Record what was useful, confusing, or unnecessary.
- Agree on the next weekly outcome.
- Build only the missing Atlas workflow that would make the next delivery
  materially faster or clearer.

## Minimum product work

The next customer-facing page should show:

- QTIME's current 30-day goal
- Three current priorities
- Open actions with owner, due date, and status
- Latest approved deliverable
- Next check-in

The minimum supporting data should be organization-scoped and include:

- a 30-day plan and next check-in;
- priorities/actions;
- deliverables; and
- separate client approval or change-request records.

Do not add CRM, automated lead generation, content publishing, billing, or AI
agents until the first customer cycle demonstrates a specific need.

## Pilot success measures

The first cycle succeeds when:

- Quincy can securely access only QTIME;
- one real weekly package is delivered and reviewed;
- Quincy identifies at least one part worth continuing;
- Manny records delivery time and direct costs;
- Atlas learns which single workflow to productize next; and
- no unapproved automation, data exposure, or surprise infrastructure spend
  occurs.
